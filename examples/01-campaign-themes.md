# Example output: campaign theme options

> What the campaign assistant returns for "Brainstorm campaign themes for Q3 from our go-to-market plan, brand narrative and customer research." Made-up campaign. It follows [`framework/campaign-planning-framework.md`](../framework/campaign-planning-framework.md) and the [theme template](../knowledge/02-brief-templates/campaign-theme-template.md). Every option traces back to a research finding, a brand pillar and an approved proof point.

## Inputs used
- **Product marketing:** [H2 go-to-market plan](../knowledge/00-strategy/2026-h2-data-quality-gtm-plan.md), [H1 customer research](../knowledge/00-strategy/customer-research-2026-h1.md)
- **Brand marketing:** [2026 brand narrative](../knowledge/00-strategy/brand-narrative-2026.md)

## Theme options

### Option 1: One customer, one record, and it stays that way
- **The assumption it challenges:** "Duplicate records are a one-time cleanup project."
- **Evidence:**
  - Research: in 11 of 18 win/loss interviews, duplicates came back within months of a cleanup.
  - Brand: the 2026 narrative is to move from "a tool for a cleanup project" to "the way your team keeps customer data clean."
  - Competitors: Clearline Data owns one-time cleanup; nobody owns "staying clean." That's the white space.
  - Approved proof points: customers cut data cleanup time by 40%; setup in under two weeks.
- **How it flexes by persona:** data leader: fewer cleanup fire drills. Analytics leader: reports that keep matching. Finance (late stage): no repeat cleanup costs.

| Test | Pass? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-channel | Yes | The video shows five records becoming one; the playbook gives the plan; the webinar shows a bank that stayed clean. |
| Ownable | Yes | It rests on monitoring, which point cleanup tools don't offer. |
| Audience-specific | Yes | Each persona gets its own reason (see above). |

- **Risks:** "stays that way" needs proof. There's no approved proof point about monitoring yet. [CONFIRM with product marketing]

### Option 2: Your reports disagree because your records do
- **The assumption it challenges:** "When reports don't match, it's a reporting tool problem."
- **Evidence:** in 8 of 12 customer calls, analytics leaders blamed the reporting tool; the cause was duplicate records. Brand pillar: Trust ("numbers that agree, wherever you look"). Proof point: teams spend 30% of their week fixing data.
- **How it flexes by persona:** strong for analytics leaders; weaker for data leaders, who already know the cause.

| Test | Pass? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-channel | Partly | Works in search, LinkedIn and email; harder to show in display and video. |
| Ownable | Partly | Any data quality vendor could say it. |
| Audience-specific | Partly | Lands with one persona, not both. |

- **Risks:** narrower; risks reaching analytics leaders who can't buy alone.

### Option 3: Cleaning it up yourself costs more than you think
- **The assumption it challenges:** "We can clean it up ourselves this quarter."
- **Evidence:** 6 of 10 lost deals went to "we'll do it ourselves," not to a competitor. Proof point: teams spend 30% of their week fixing data.
- **How it flexes by persona:** strong for finance leaders; weaker for data leaders, who may hear it as "your team can't do it."

| Test | Pass? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-channel | Yes | A cost message works in every channel. |
| Ownable | No | Every vendor makes a cost argument. |
| Audience-specific | Partly | Finance only, and finance joins late. |

- **Risks:** drifts toward fear-based messaging, which the brand guidelines rule out.

## Recommendation
Option 1. It's the only one that passes all three tests, and it lines up with both the brand's 2026 direction and the white space in the competitor review. Use Option 2 as the message for analytics leaders inside the campaign, and keep Option 3's cost point for late-stage finance conversations only.
